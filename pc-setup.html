Set up the house cameras on your PC

This page puts a small free program on your PC. The program takes the pictures from your camera unit and hands them to the camera page, so you can watch your ten cameras in your browser. Part One is a short test. Part Two — watching the cameras — comes after the test passes.

Every file in this setup goes in your Downloads folder, the folder already on your PC. No new folder gets made. The files sit together in that one place for one reason: each starter file looks for the program and its settings right beside itself, so they have to stand together. When all of this is done, Downloads holds the translator program, its settings file, and the start file — they stay while you use the viewer. The test files can be deleted once the test is done.

How to save a file from this page, the same way every time: right-click the file name, click Save link as…, then in the window that opens click Downloads on the left of the window, and click Save. A plain click on a file name does not save it — it shows the file's words on the screen. If that happens, go back and use the right-click.

Part One — the test

The test asks your camera unit two things: is its password still blank, and which address form does it answer to? It takes a few minutes.

  1. Get the translator program. Open the maker's page: go2rtc releases page. The newest release stands at the top of that page. In its list of files — click Assets first if the list is folded shut — click go2rtc_win64.zip and let it save. Open the saved zip and drag the program inside it, go2rtc.exe, into your Downloads folder.
  2. Save the two test files into your Downloads folder, the way the note at the top says:
    test-config.yaml — the test settings. Your unit's address and the blank password are already written in. There is nothing to change.
    run-test.bat — the test starter. This is the file you double-click.
  3. Run the test. Double-click run-test.bat in your Downloads folder. A black window opens — leave it open the whole time. In a few seconds a page opens in your browser: a table with five lines, one for each test. Each line holds a name on the left — test-form1, test-form2, and so on — and three words at the right end of the line: stream, links, delete.
    If a blue window says Windows protected your PC, click More info, then click Run anyway. That window is Windows being careful with a program it has not seen before.
  4. Wake each test — the click that matters. On the line for test-form1, click the word stream at the right end of that line. A new page opens and tries to show camera 1's picture. Count to ten and look: either a real camera picture, or a black square. Then click your browser's Back button to return to the table, and do the same on the next line — stream, count to ten, Back — through test-form5.
    Clicking a name itself only ticks the small box beside it and starts nothing. The tick boxes play no part in this test — leave them alone.
  5. Send the pictures. When you are back at the table, send Muggy a picture of that page, and a picture of the black window. Then stop here — what comes next depends on the pictures.

What the pictures will say:

  1. A real camera picture opened on any line — that address form is the road, and the blank password is right. Part Two comes next, when Muggy says so.
  2. The number 401 in the black window — a password stands on the unit, and the blank one is not it. Stop and tell Muggy.
  3. The words actively refused in the black window — the unit answered, but its camera-stream switch stands off. Go to Part Three.

Part Two — watch the cameras

Do this part only after Muggy says the test passed.

  1. Save the two working files into your Downloads folder, the same way as before:
    go2rtc.yaml — the settings for all ten cameras. The address and login are already written in. There is nothing to change.
    start-cameras.bat — the start file.
  2. Start it. Double-click start-cameras.bat. A black window opens — that is the translator running. Leave it open the whole time you watch. Chrome opens the house camera page on its own, with your ten cameras live.
  3. Sound. The page has a Sound button in its top bar. Turn it on, then tap one camera to make it big — that camera's sound plays. With one camera big, a Hold to talk button stands there too; whether your unit passes talk-back is settled by trying it.
  4. Stop it. Close the black window. That is all — the sending stops, and the camera page has no pictures until you start again.

Part Three — only if the test said “actively refused”

Your unit has a switch that lets it hand out camera pictures, and it ships turned off. One tick turns it on.

  1. Go to the unit and look at its own screen. Right-click on the screen and open System Setup.
  2. Open Network Setup, then Network Service.
  3. Tick Enable RTSP Server, then click Apply.

Your unit's screens may use slightly different words — its own book calls these pages System setting and Network setting. If you cannot find a Network Service page, do not hunt for it. Send Muggy a picture of the Network Setup screen and stop there.

Then run the second test, the same way as the first:

  1. Save the two second-test files into your Downloads folder:
    test2-config.yaml — the second test settings, already filled in.
    run-test2.bat — the second test starter.
  2. Double-click run-test2.bat. The black window opens — leave it open — and the browser page opens with the second test's five lines.
  3. Wake each test, the same click as the first test: on each line, click the word stream at the right end of the line, count to ten, then click Back — through test2-form1 to test2-form5. Clicking a name only ticks a box; leave the boxes alone.
  4. Send the pictures — the table page, and the black window — and stop there.
