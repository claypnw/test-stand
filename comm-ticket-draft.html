Communication ticket — the draft form DRAFT

What the research found: ITIL 4 does not print one fixed ticket form. It names what a record must carry: a number no other ticket uses, the time it was opened, who sent it, what it is about in one line, the full words, who holds it, its state, the answer, and how it was closed.

The big tools carry the same core set. ServiceNow and Jira hold near the same fields — number, one-line subject, the full words, state, sender, holder, a drop-down for the kind, the back-and-forth in one place, and the things the ticket names. Their full set runs past 40 fields; this form keeps 14 and cuts the rest, because four AIs pass these notes, not a help desk with a long queue.

The Google Form road does not fit as the ticket's home. A form does gather answers in one set shape, with working drop-downs, and lands them as rows in a Sheet. But a form is one-way — the receiver cannot answer inside the same entry — and a Sheet row is a hard thing to read as a ticket. So the ticket is a file shaped like a form, and it travels in the hand-off folder, a road all four AIs can already walk.

This page is a draft for Clay's eyes. The form below does not send anywhere — the drop-downs and boxes work so the shape can be judged, and nothing filled in here is kept or sent. Nothing on this page is in force.
